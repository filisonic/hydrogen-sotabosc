MIRA · Fira Montjuïc · 6–7 November

Eyes that look back

Fifteen editions in, Barcelona’s digital arts festival handed perception to the machines and asked what they see.

MIRA has spent fifteen years putting digital art on the same bill as contemporary music, in a city that watched the medium grow up. This year the art programme had eleven installations and performances, sharing two days with a twenty-seven act music line-up.

The theme was easy to read: post-human ecosystems, and perception handed over to machines. Pinar Yoldas showed a speculative ecology built to survive inside the Great Pacific Garbage Patch. Sarabiotic’s environment, drawn from the ocean floor, shifted as people walked past. Andreas Lutz built a system that learns from the faces it meets.

Then the body entered the interface. AEDOSSYNTH gave three artificial heads a voice; Onionlab turned the main stage into a surface of light that answered the sound.

The quieter story was the schools. Students from EINA, ELISAVA, IED Barcelona and La Salle showed work beside the headliners. Fifteen years of that has built a pipeline from Barcelona’s design schools onto the festival floor.
