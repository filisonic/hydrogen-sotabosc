The city gets a screen

Barcelona’s first Biennial of Digital Arts opens with forty works at DHub, and plans a screen for art in a metro hall.

Words by the Sotabosc desk

For a long time, digital art in Barcelona lived in festivals: two loud days at a fairground, then gone until next year. This week the city tried something slower. The first Biennial of Digital Arts opened at the Disseny Hub on 25 November and will run, in pieces, until March.

Its first exhibition is called “Vectors. Digital art to think about the future”. Curated by Irma Vidal i Òdena, it brings together around forty works by thirty-three artists, and it does something festivals rarely have room for: it puts the pioneers in the same room as the people they made possible.

So Vera Molnár, Elena Asins, Manfred Mohr, John Whitney and Lillian Schwartz, who were drawing with machines before most of us had seen one, hang alongside Mario Klingemann, Joana Moll, Mónica Rikić, Anna Carreras, Alba G. Corral, Hamill Industries, Playmodes and Zachary Lieberman.

The Biennial is directed by Pep Salazar and organised by the City Council, the Generalitat’s culture department and the Barcelona Creativity & Design Foundation, with money from the Ministry of Culture.
