A set of generative science pieces for a 4K wall display makes a simple promise: nothing on screen is animated. Each piece is a single HTML file that simulates something real and draws what happens. A run that goes badly is allowed to go badly, because none of it is scripted.

Four thousand starlings turn over a field at dusk with nobody leading. About 137,000 particles behave like slime mould and grow a transport network. A survey car hunts methane leaks, and the colour on the street is not the gas but the survey’s growing belief about where it is.

Others draw on real data: lightning from a weather satellite, the 4,737 nearest stars known to carry planets, artworks pulled live from the Art Institute of Chicago’s open collection. All of it is free to open and take apart.

A screen can show the world working, not just a picture of it. This is the idea behind Sotabosc TV’s interludes: art that is drawn live, so the room never sees the same minute twice. Sotabosc journal, 11 October. Pieces: WorldByJoe, tv-art-display on GitHub
Did you know A coral can be brought back by touch

A Stanford team placed bleached and healthy coral of the same genetic line in contact. Within a week, sometimes three days, the healthy partner’s symbionts crossed over and the pale one filled back with colour. It is a lab result, not a reef method yet, but it turns surviving patches into a source rather than a remnant.
